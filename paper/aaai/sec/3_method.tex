\section{Method}
RelCompat3D re-ranks fixed relation predictions rather than replacing a 3D scene graph generator. It preserves the ordered-pair identity of each candidate, estimates predicate--geometry compatibility without the source relation score, and combines the two only during re-ranking. We first define the compatibility inputs, then describe the two estimators and the family-aware re-ranking rule. Figure~\ref{fig:overall_framework} presents an overview of RelCompat3D.

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
Let a relation predictor produce candidates for a 3D scene. Each candidate is written as $r_i=(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i,p_i,a_i,Z_i)$.
$s_i$ and $o_i$ are ordered subject and object instance identifiers, $p_i$ is the predicate label, $a_i$ is the mapped relation family, and $Z_i$ is the source relation score. We use sigmoid relation scores for VL-SAT~\cite{wang2023vl} and SGFN~\cite{wu2021scenegraphfusion}. For Open3DSG~\cite{koch2024open3dsg}, we use cosine similarity between normalized text embeddings. We rank candidates separately for each predictor without predictor-specific normalization or refitting. We do not compare scores across predictors.

A context is a scene subgraph over which top-K selection is performed independently. We distinguish the ordered-pair identity $k^{\rm pair}_i = (\mathrm{scan}_i, \mathrm{context}_i, s_i, o_i)$ from the exact relation-candidate identity $k^{\rm rel}_i = (\mathrm{scan}_i, \mathrm{context}_i, s_i, p_i, o_i)$ used for evaluation. We retain ordered-pair identity when associating predictions with geometry and ground truth.

Let $T_i=p_i$ denote predicate semantics. The family label $a_i$ selects the applicable transformations and determines whether the candidate is re-ranked. For the Linear estimator, it also selects the compatibility head and training-split normalization statistics. The shared nonlinear estimator instead encodes the family as an input indicator. We associate each candidate with predicate-independent ordered-pair measurements $G_i=G(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i)$. We evaluate $\mathcal{A}_{\rm eval}=\{\mathrm{support/contact},\ \mathrm{proximity},\ \mathrm{vertical\ order}\}$, whose consistency can be assessed from reconstructed ordered-pair geometry. Candidates outside $\mathcal{A}_{\rm eval}$ are excluded from the reported Recall and Violation evaluation. Re-ranking relation families are narrower: $\mathcal A_{\rm rank}=\{\mathrm{proximity},\mathrm{vertical\ order}\}$, for which endpoint/predicate transformations are defined. Support/contact is evaluated but kept in source order because it requires richer local contact and pose evidence. No endpoint transformation preserves all predicate semantics in this family.

For estimator $q\in\{\mathrm{lin},\mathrm{mlp}\}$, our objective is to estimate a bounded compatibility score
\begin{equation}
C_i^q=\sigma\!\left(f_q(T_i,a_i,G_i)\right)\in[0,1].
\end{equation}
Here $\sigma$ is the logistic sigmoid. We average compatibility over applicable family-specific transformations to obtain $C_i^{\mathrm{tr},q}$. The source relation score enters only the within-family ranking score. $C_i^q$ is a compatibility score used for ranking, not a posterior probability of geometric validity. Training-split ground truth provides positives. Proximity negatives use pairs from the same context that are distant and do not overlap. Vertical-order negatives keep the endpoints fixed and invert the predicate. Separately, relation-preserving augmentation swaps proximity endpoints without changing the predicate and jointly swaps vertical endpoints with the inverse predicate. We exclude evaluation rows, source relation scores, and verifier-status labels from training example construction.

\subsection{Compatibility Estimation}
\label{sec:Compatibility}
For each candidate $r_i$, $G_i$ contains oriented bounding box (OBB)-derived 3D/XY distances, relative height, projected overlap, and vertical-gap features. Point-level contact evidence is used only by the support/contact verifier and excluded from the compatibility model.

Let $\operatorname{std}_{\rm tr}$ standardize each numeric feature with training-split statistics and replace a missing value by the corresponding training mean. Both estimators use the same training targets, loss, and transformation averaging.

\subsubsection{Linear Estimator.}
The feature map for RelCompat3D-Linear is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{\rm lin}(T_i,G_i)&=[1;\phi_T(T_i);\operatorname{std}_{\rm tr}(G_i);
\operatorname{std}_{\rm tr}(\phi_{\times}(T_i,G_i))],\\
\phi_{\times}(T_i,G_i)&=
[d(p_i)\Delta z_i,\ d(p_i)\Delta z_i^{\rm norm}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let $z_{s_i}$ and $z_{o_i}$ denote the vertical coordinates of the subject and object OBB centers, and let $h_{s_i}$ and $h_{o_i}$ denote their OBB heights. We define $\Delta z_i=z_{s_i}-z_{o_i}$ and $\Delta z_i^{\rm norm}=2\Delta z_i / (h_{s_i}+h_{o_i})$.
Here $\phi_T$ contains predicate indicators, and $d(p_i)$ is $1$ for \texttt{higher than}, $-1$ for \texttt{lower than}, and $0$ otherwise. The family label $a_i$ selects the linear head and training-split normalization statistics but is not included as a constant input: $f_{\rm lin}(T_i,a_i,G_i)=w_{a_i}^\top\Phi_{\rm lin}(T_i,G_i)$.

\subsubsection{Nonlinear Estimator.}
RelCompat3D-MLP uses a shared multilayer perceptron (MLP) with a two-unit ReLU hidden layer. Its input $\Psi_i$ contains family and predicate indicators, the same underlying geometric measurements, the two signed-height interactions above, and the sum of the two directional overlap ratios. With a predicate-linear skip connection, its logit is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h_i&=\operatorname{ReLU}(W\Psi_i+b),\\
f_{\rm mlp}(T_i,a_i,G_i)&=v^\top h_i+b_o+
\beta^\top\phi_T(T_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here \(W\) and \(v\) are learned weights, \(b\) and \(b_o\) are biases,
and \(\beta\) weights the predicate-indicator skip path. The skip connection provides a direct linear path from predicate indicators to the output logit. The nonlinear estimator is shared across families, so its family indicator is not a constant feature. Both estimators exclude the source relation score, rank, predictor identity, and object-class features.

Let $H_a$ denote the finite transformation group for family $a$. For proximity and vertical order, $H_a$ contains the identity and the family-specific transformation $\tau_a$, defined as follows. For proximity, $\tau_a$ swaps the ordered endpoints and leaves \texttt{close by} unchanged. For vertical order, $\tau_a$ swaps the endpoints and maps \texttt{higher than} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{lower than}. For support/contact, $H_a$ contains only the identity transformation. The support/contact compatibility head is used only for the Product (all families) comparison.
Training combines this augmentation with a linked positive--counterfactual ranking loss. For every linked positive--counterfactual pair $(i^+,i^-)\in\mathcal P$, the logits $\ell_i^q=f_q(T_i,a_i,G_i)$ receive a margin penalty:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_q={}&\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}
+\lambda_{\rm pair}\,\mathbb E_{\mathcal P}
\left[\operatorname{softplus}
\left(m-(\ell^q_{i^+}-\ell^q_{i^-})\right)\right]\\
&+\lambda_{\rm reg}\mathcal R(\theta_q).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}$ is binary cross-entropy over positive and counterfactual examples. The second term is a softplus margin-ranking loss with margin $m$. We set $m=1$, $\lambda_{\rm pair}=0.25$, and $\lambda_{\rm reg}=10^{-4}$ in all experiments. These values are shared across estimators and predictors without predictor-specific search. $\mathcal R$ is an $\ell_2$ penalty on the non-bias parameters in $\theta_q$, where $\theta_q$ denotes the trainable parameters of estimator $q$. Both estimators are fitted exclusively on the training split.

For candidate $i$, define the transformation orbit $\mathcal O_i=\{g(T_i,G_i):g\in H_{a_i}\}$. We average the learned score over this set to obtain the transformation-consistent compatibility:
\begin{equation}
C_i^{\rm tr,q}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal O_i|}
\sum_{(T',G')\in\mathcal O_i} \sigma\!\left(f_q(T',a_i,G')\right).
\label{eq:algebra-projection}
\end{equation}
Transformation averaging therefore yields
$C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm close}(s,o)=C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm close}(o,s)$ and
$C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm higher}(s,o)=C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm lower}(o,s)$. Group averaging makes proximity symmetry and invariance under joint endpoint swap and inverse-predicate transformation exact at inference. The pairwise objective encourages each linked positive to score above its counterfactual.

\subsection{Family-Aware Re-Ranking}
For each compatibility estimator $q$, define the within-family ranking score
\begin{equation}
u_i^q=\begin{cases}
Z_iC_i^{\rm tr,q}, & a_i\in\mathcal A_{\rm rank},\\
Z_i, & a_i=\mathrm{support/contact}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
Let $\pi^Z=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ denote the source ranking, and let $a_j$ be the relation family at position $j$. For each $a\in\mathcal A_{\rm rank}$, let $\pi_a^q$ contain the candidates in family $a$ sorted by $u_i^q$, with ties resolved by exact relation-candidate identity. For support/contact, $\pi_a^q$ is the corresponding family subsequence of $\pi^Z$. Thus, a candidate can exchange positions only with candidates from the same re-ranked family. At position $j$, the rule selects the next unused candidate from $\pi_{a_j}^q$. The output therefore preserves the source relation-family sequence and the support/contact subsequence at every $K$. Rank-fusion baselines use the same ranking procedure with alternative score combinations. The supplement provides the complete counterfactual rules, optimization details, and proofs.
