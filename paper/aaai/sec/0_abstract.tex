\begin{abstract}
3D Scene Graphs represent objects and relations in a form useful for spatial
reasoning, but relation predictions can remain unreliable even when predicted
predicates appear semantically plausible. We study a relation-level failure
mode: semantic relation confidence is not necessarily calibrated to the 3D
geometry of the same object pair, so a predictor can rank physically
inconsistent relations highly. To expose and reduce this failure, we introduce
a \method{} framework for geometry-checkable relation families. The framework
factorizes predicate semantics $T$, predicate-independent same-pair geometry
$G$, and source confidence $Z$; it calibrates a constructed-target
compatibility $C=P(y^{cal}=1\mid T,G)$ without using $Z$, then reports
$F(Z,C)$ under a recall-violation protocol. Two soft fusion instantiations use
a calibrated product and a scale-robust rank-average; pooled calibration,
Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and hard rules serve as ablation, comparison, and
diagnostic. On the full
official validation split, the calibrated product improves VL-SAT
\rAt{50}/\rAt{100} from 0.9272/0.9635 to 0.9288/0.9683 while reducing
\vAt{100} from 0.0476 to 0.0333. On the main
open-vocabulary relation-source case study, the Open3DSG full-validation
recovery branch, the product improves
\rAt{100} from 0.5161 to 0.6047 while reducing \vAt{100} from 0.1242 to 0.0341 under explicit
selected-checkpoint and recovery-policy caveats. On a prospectively frozen
SGFN target, both the product and pre-specified rank-average pass the joint
\rAt{100}/verifier-\vAt{100} gate, showing that the aggregate effect is not
tied to one fusion formula. GT positive/counterfactual checks
separate valid and invalid geometry with AUROC/AUPRC of 0.9772/0.9729, and
controls rule out calibrator-only, distance-only, shuffled-geometry, and
wrong-pair explanations. Here $y^{cal}$ denotes train-dev GT positives and
high-margin counterfactual negatives, not direct human validity. These results support a scoped
relation-reliability claim for measured geometry-checkable 3D Scene Graph
relations, not formula dominance or broad open-vocabulary graph generation.
\end{abstract}
