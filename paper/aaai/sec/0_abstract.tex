\begin{abstract}
3D Scene Graph predictors can assign high scores to relations that sound
plausible but fail explicit geometry tests for the predicted object pair. We
study this mismatch as a calibration problem: a source relation score ranks
candidates but need not estimate predicate-conditioned geometric consistency. We
introduce \method{}, a reliability framework for geometry-checkable relations.
It separates predicate semantics $T$, predicate-independent same-pair geometry
$G$, and source score $Z$; fits a predicate-conditioned compatibility
$C(T,G)$ without access to $Z$; and combines $Z$ and $C$ only when re-ranking.
We instantiate the framework with a family-calibrated product and a
parameter-free rank-average fusion, and evaluate both with exact-label
Recall@$K$ and verifier Violation@$K$. On the Open3DSG source, the calibrated
product improves Recall@100 from 0.5161 to 0.6047 while reducing Violation@100
from 0.1242 to 0.0341. At the primary $K=100$ endpoint, both evaluated
fusion rules satisfy the joint recall--violation criterion on SGFN. The calibrated
product remains effective at smaller
operating budgets, including $K=50$ and $K=10$, while factor-isolation,
counterfactual, and object-pair identity controls reject score-copying and
distance-only explanations. Across three predictors on a fixed 3DSSG target,
the results support source-level generalization for geometry-identifiable
relations; support/contact and transfer to a different dataset remain open.
\end{abstract}
