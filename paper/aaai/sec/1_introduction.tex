\section{Introduction}

3D scene graphs represent reconstructed scenes as structured objects and relations for spatial reasoning and embodied perception \cite{armeni20193d,wald2020learning}. They also support scene alignment~\cite{sarkar2023sgaligner,xie2024sg} and visual grounding \cite{liu2026view}. A predicted relation must describe the corresponding ordered subject--object pair in 3D, not merely appear plausible at the category or language level.

We study cases in which relation predictors assign high scores to predicates that contradict the measured distance, projected overlap, or vertical arrangement of the ordered pair. This mismatch becomes consequential as 3D scene graphs become interfaces for open-vocabulary applications and embodied systems \cite{koch2024open3dsg,hou2025fross,zhang2025open,wang2025open,saxena2025zing,fei2026open,Madhavaram_2026_WACV}. Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} shows this mismatch. One structural source of this risk is the use of text-embedding similarity for open-vocabulary ranking. Cosine similarity measures semantic similarity but does not explicitly estimate predicate--geometry compatibility for the corresponding ordered pair.

Existing predictors already use geometric, edge, and point-cloud evidence \cite{wald2020learning,zhang2021exploiting}. Other methods add multimodal, self-supervised, or open-vocabulary cues \cite{feng20233d,wang2023vl,koch2024sgrec3d,chen2024clip}. Geometry may inform a predictor without making its source relation score an explicit estimate of predicate--geometry compatibility for the same ordered pair. This motivates separating predicate semantics $T$, ordered-pair measurements $G$, and the source relation score $Z$.

We propose RelCompat3D, which preserves the ordered-pair identity of each candidate and learns predicate--geometry compatibility from $T$ and $G$ without using $Z$ or predictor identity. We formulate this separation with family-specific linear heads and a shared nonlinear estimator. Both estimators are trained with linked positive--counterfactual pairs so that geometry-consistent candidates outrank their incompatible counterparts. Counterfactuals create incompatible training pairs, whereas the transformations encode alternative representations of the same relation semantics. We average compatibility over endpoint swaps, keeping proximity predicates fixed and inverting the predicate for vertical order. This assigns equal compatibility to relation-preserving variants. Excluding $Z$ prevents direct copying of the source relation score. Preserving ordered-pair identity ensures that compatibility is computed from the geometry of the corresponding pair.

The source relation score is used only during re-ranking. Within proximity and vertical-order relations, RelCompat3D combines it with compatibility and changes the order within each relation family. It preserves the relation-family label sequence and support/contact order from the source ranking. Matched fusion baselines and controls examine the roles of the source relation score, ordered-pair identity, and transformation consistency.

We evaluate fixed predictions from Open3DSG~\cite{koch2024open3dsg}, VL-SAT~\cite{wang2023vl}, and a SceneGraphFusion benchmark model (SGFN)~\cite{wu2021scenegraphfusion} on the official 3DSSG validation split. These predictors provide complementary open-vocabulary and closed-set relation predictions. We report exact-match Recall@$K$ and verifier-derived Violation@$K$ on the shared 3DSSG validation scenes. Across all reported $K$ values, both variants match or improve Recall point estimates and match or reduce Violation point estimates relative to Source. The controls show that source relation scores and compatibility affect each predictor ranking differently. Compatibility-only ordering nearly matches the linear variant in Recall on Open3DSG, while source relation scores remain important for preserving Recall on VL-SAT and SGFN. A separate point- and mesh-based audit evaluates the same rankings using alternative geometric measurements.

In summary, our contributions are threefold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We define and measure a mismatch between source relation scores and ordered-pair geometric compatibility using exact-match Recall@$K$ and verifier-derived Violation@$K$.
    \item We estimate predicate--geometry compatibility without the source relation score and enforce exact consistency under applicable endpoint and predicate transformations.
    \item We introduce family-aware re-ranking and characterize its predictor- and relation-family-dependent behavior across three fixed predictors.
\end{enumerate}
