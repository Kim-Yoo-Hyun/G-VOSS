\section{Capacity, Fusion, and Open3DSG Sensitivities}

\paragraph{Compatibility capacity and matched fusion.}
\methodlinear{} uses family-specific linear heads, whereas \methodmlp{} uses a
shared two-hidden-unit ReLU model with a predicate-linear skip connection. They
share the constructed training target, linked-pair objective, transformation
averaging, exclusion of predictor scores and identities, product utility, and
family-aware ranking. The MLP input includes a family indicator because its
parameters are shared, plus an overlap-sum interaction derived from the same
directional overlap measurements. Both are fitted once and applied to all three
predictors without target-specific refitting. Rank-average fusion (RankAvg) and
reciprocal rank fusion (RRF) use the linear compatibility scores and the same
family-aware ranking procedure.

Let $q_i^Z$ and $q_i^C$ denote descending within-context percentile ranks of
the predictor score and compatibility, and let $r_i^Z,r_i^C$ be their
one-indexed ranks. Rank-average and fixed-constant Reciprocal Rank Fusion use
\begin{equation}
S_i^{\mathrm{rank}}=\tfrac12(q_i^Z+q_i^C),\qquad
S_i^{\mathrm{RRF}}=\frac{1}{60+r_i^Z}+\frac{1}{60+r_i^C}.
\end{equation}

At $K=50$, the nonlinear variant has higher Open3DSG Recall than the linear
variant but also higher V; they are nearly tied on VL-SAT and SGFN. Rank fusion can
lower V at large budgets but incurs larger predictor-dependent Recall losses.
No estimator or fusion dominates the joint trade-off. A separate exact-match MLP uses
stronger SGFN-specific correctness supervision and is therefore retained only
as a source-specific upper comparator, not as an apples-to-apples baseline.
