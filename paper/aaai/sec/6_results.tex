\subsection{Cross-Predictor Recall--Violation Trade-off}

Table~\ref{tab:main-results} and Figure~\ref{fig:tradeoff} show that the
applicability-routed \method{} improves both metrics at $K=100$ on all three
predictors, with paired scan-cluster intervals excluding zero for each Recall
and V contrast. Open3DSG exposes the failure most strongly:
the source ranks geometrically inconsistent edges at small $K$, whereas the
route changes both recall and violation rather than only pruning edges.
Across all reported budgets, neither routed point estimate is worse than its
source counterpart; SGFN is unchanged at $K=5$. Near-ceiling VL-SAT and SGFN
leave less recall headroom but retain stable violation reductions.

No single comparator dominates across all predictors and operating budgets.
The unrestricted product reaches
higher Recall than the route but changes support/contact selections; pooled
product reaches the highest Open3DSG Recall@100 at substantially higher V.
Rank fusion also incurs predictor-dependent Recall losses. The route is the
only proposed condition that preserves family composition and support/contact
selection exactly while improving the aggregate endpoint.

The primary scan-cluster analysis moves all contexts from a sampled scan
together. All routed Recall intervals remain above zero and every V interval
remains below zero (supplement); a paired context-bootstrap sensitivity gives
the same directions.

On the development split, linked positives outrank their counterfactuals in
99.23\% of pairs, and correct vertical predicates outrank their inverses in
97.40\% of cases. Projection makes proximity-swap and vertical-inverse errors
exactly zero on development and across every evaluation row. These results
verify the implemented algebra and support the intended counterfactual ordering.

The unprojected product has nearly identical aggregate Recall, while projection
removes the applicable algebra errors exactly. The factor ablation reports
$T$-only, true raw-$G$-only, additive, and interaction conditions in the
supplement. A supervision-matched nonlinear compatibility model does not
dominate the linear structured model: on Open3DSG it gains Recall but
significantly increases V, while differences are small on VL-SAT and SGFN. The
stronger SGFN-specific exact-label rescorer improves its source endpoint but
does not dominate when applied unchanged to the other predictors (supplement).

Table~\ref{tab:ablations} applies every perturbation through the primary route.
The corruptions consistently break the joint operating point. Wrong-pair and
shuffled geometry show that the gain depends on the identity-preserving join;
wrong predicates and label-fixed swaps sharply increase V. Removing the source
score inside routed families can retain low V but loses substantial Recall,
especially on VL-SAT and SGFN, confirming that geometry does not replace source
confidence. The unrestricted mechanism audit is reported in the supplement.

Uncertainty sensitivity gives the same aggregate conclusion: decidable-only
and pessimistic V decrease at $K=100$ on every predictor. Uncertainty changes
slightly in either direction, so the all-status improvement is not explained
by uniformly moving failures into uncertainty.

\subsection{Qualitative and Family Analysis}

Figure~\ref{fig:failures} shows a routed proximity correction and the
support/contact boundary. The route moves the heater--trash-can proximity edge
from rank 19 to 178, while retaining the two support candidates at their source
ranks. The unrestricted product would move one from 39 to 337 but retains the
other at rank 10, illustrating why its support behavior is not reliable enough
for the primary route.

Family-wise intervals prevent aggregate overstatement. Relative vertical
supplies most V reduction, proximity improves the global selection, and
support/contact is exactly unchanged by construction. The method therefore
does not claim to solve contact-heavy relations;
full within-family and global-top-$K$ composition tables are provided in the
supplement.
