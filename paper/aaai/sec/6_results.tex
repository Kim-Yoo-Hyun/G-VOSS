The central question is whether calibrated geometry consistency reduces
physically inconsistent relation predictions without collapsing recall. We
begin with Open3DSG because it is the main open-vocabulary source closest to
the intended use case. Table~\ref{tab:source-results} shows that Open3DSG
\texttt{semantic\_only} reaches \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} of 0.3945/0.4963 with
\vAt{50}/\vAt{100} of 0.1326/0.1195. The
\texttt{probabilistic} setting reduces violations to 0.0575/0.0803 while
shifting recall to 0.3843/0.5580. The \texttt{family\_specific} operating
point gives the strongest Open3DSG tradeoff, reaching 0.4530/0.5984 recall and
0.0228/0.0311 violation. This is the main open-vocabulary reliability evidence
for H001, with the reproduction and coverage caveats fixed in
Table~\ref{tab:claim-boundary}.

VL-SAT provides a controlled anchor for the same row contract and metric
protocol. Its \texttt{semantic\_only} ranking reaches \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} of
0.9599/0.9894 with \vAt{50}/\vAt{100} of 0.0247/0.0469. The
\texttt{probabilistic} setting improves \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} to 0.9642/0.9921
and reduces \vAt{100} to 0.0391, while the \texttt{family\_specific} setting
reduces \vAt{100} to 0.0310. The \texttt{rule\_verified} rows on both sources
are zero-violation diagnostics rather than the default method, because hard
filtering exposes one end of the reliability-recall tradeoff.

The nontriviality controls rule out simpler alternatives. Geometry-only ranking
falls to \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} of 0.2028/0.5049 with \vAt{100}=0.0701, and
distance-only ranking reaches only 0.3835/0.5642 with \vAt{100}=0.0993.
Shuffled-geometry and wrong-pair controls keep more source signal
(\rAt{100}=0.9788 for both), but still degrade violations to 0.0559 and 0.0581
relative to calibrated identity-preserving joins. These controls support the
design requirement that geometry evidence must be joined to the correct object
pair and calibrated as a relation-level reliability signal.

The GT-based verifier check tests the geometry signal independently from
prediction re-ranking. GT-positive rows remain nonviolated at a rate of 0.9972,
while GT-derived counterfactual negatives are nonsatisfied at a rate of 0.9694.
The calibrated \pgeom{} score separates these 5,090 rows with AUROC/AUPRC of
0.9779/0.9737 and Brier score 0.0538. This supports the use of \pgeom{} as a
reliability signal while still leaving room for residual calibration risk.

Structured audit and visual sanity checks support the failure interpretation,
but they are not reported as large-scale independent human evaluation. The
structured audit covers 250 rows and reaches quality-issue precision of
0.8933. The reduced 50-row visual spot-check, reviewer-confirmed by
\texttt{yhkim}, reaches a target-bucket quality-issue rate of 0.9333 with
contradiction rate 0.0333 and private-reference match 1.0000. These checks are
used to detect annotation or verifier issues and to support failure-mechanism
interpretation, not as primary metric evidence.

Together, Table~\ref{tab:source-results} and the control/verifier checks
support a calibrated reliability-layer interpretation. They do not show that
H001 is a new relation generator, that it improves all open-vocabulary relation
labels, or that it is baseline-agnostic across arbitrary 3DSSG predictors.
Those blocked extensions are fixed in Table~\ref{tab:claim-boundary}.

The qualitative failure analysis explains why the Open3DSG metric changes
occur. Open3DSG produces 57,736 real analysis rows with zero validation errors,
and the inspected 36-case queue shows that 23 cases are demoted by
geometry-aware re-ranking. Figure~\ref{fig:failures} shows family-structured
failures: proximity failures often involve distance contradictions,
relative-vertical failures involve vertical-order contradictions, and
support/contact failures expose float-gap or support-plane contradictions.

The same qualitative analysis also exposes a limitation. Ten of the 36 sampled
rule-violated cases still have $\pgeom > 0.9$. This means the calibrated score
should not be interpreted as a hard validity label. It also justifies why the
paper reports probabilistic, rule-verified, and family-specific variants
separately rather than collapsing them into a single geometry-corrected output.
