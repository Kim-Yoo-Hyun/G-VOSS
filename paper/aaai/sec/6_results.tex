The central question is whether calibrated geometry consistency reduces
physically inconsistent relation predictions without collapsing recall. We
begin with Open3DSG because it is the main open-vocabulary relation-source
closest to the intended use case. Table~\ref{tab:source-results} shows that
Open3DSG \texttt{semantic\_only} reaches \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} of 0.4096/0.5161 with
\vAt{50}/\vAt{100} of 0.1386/0.1242. The
\texttt{probabilistic} setting reduces violations to 0.0606/0.0811 while
shifting recall to 0.3975/0.5723. The \texttt{family\_specific} operating
point gives the strongest Open3DSG tradeoff, reaching 0.4658/0.6047 recall and
0.0286/0.0341 violation. This is the main source-output reliability evidence
for the open-vocabulary setting, with the recovery-policy and source
provenance caveats fixed in Table~\ref{tab:claim-boundary}.

VL-SAT provides a controlled anchor for the same row contract and metric
protocol. Its \texttt{semantic\_only} ranking reaches \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} of
0.9272/0.9635 with \vAt{50}/\vAt{100} of 0.0268/0.0476. The
\texttt{probabilistic} setting improves \rAt{50}/\rAt{100} to 0.9305/0.9688
and reduces \vAt{100} to 0.0404, while the \texttt{family\_specific} setting
reduces \vAt{100} to 0.0333. The \texttt{rule\_verified} rows on both sources
are zero-violation diagnostics rather than the default method, because hard
filtering exposes one end of the reliability-recall tradeoff.

A Docker subgraph bootstrap with 1,000 resamples gives the Open3DSG
\texttt{family\_specific} operating point a \rAt{100} delta of +8.86
percentage points (95\% CI +6.69,+10.96) and a \vAt{100} delta of -9.01
points (95\% CI -9.49,-8.53) relative to \texttt{semantic\_only}. VL-SAT
deltas are smaller but still favorable: \texttt{family\_specific} changes
\rAt{100} by +0.48 points (95\% CI +0.11,+0.93) and reduces \vAt{100} by
-1.43 points (95\% CI -1.60,-1.28). We use these intervals as
evaluation-context uncertainty checks, not as repeated-training variance.

The nontriviality controls rule out simpler alternatives. On the VL-SAT
controlled anchor, using the same 3,972-row in-scope GT denominator as
Table~\ref{tab:source-results}, geometry-only ranking falls to
\rAt{50}/\rAt{100} of 0.2110/0.5184 with \vAt{100}=0.0711, and distance-only
ranking reaches only 0.3746/0.5554 with \vAt{100}=0.0981. Shuffled-geometry
and wrong-pair controls keep more source signal (\rAt{100}=0.9494/0.9529),
but still degrade violations to 0.0588 and 0.0601 relative to calibrated
identity-preserving joins. The Open3DSG source-output case study shows the same
need for identity-preserving relation geometry: distance-only reaches
\rAt{100}=0.5038 and \vAt{100}=0.1071, geometry-only reaches 0.5116 and
0.0865, while shuffled-geometry and wrong-pair controls fall to
\rAt{100}=0.2543/0.2331 with \vAt{100}=0.1998/0.1985. These controls support
the design requirement that geometry evidence must be joined to the correct
object pair and calibrated as a relation-level reliability signal. They also
rule out the strongest simple-baseline objection: H001 is not merely ranking by
geometry, distance, or hard rules, because those variants either lose recall,
leave more violations, or fail when object-pair identity is broken.

The GT-based verifier check tests the geometry signal independently from
prediction re-ranking. GT-positive rows remain nonviolated at a rate of 0.9965,
while GT-derived counterfactual negatives are nonsatisfied at a rate of 0.9673.
The calibrated \pgeom{} score separates these 7,944 rows with AUROC/AUPRC of
0.9772/0.9729 and Brier score 0.0543. This supports the use of \pgeom{} as a
reliability signal while still leaving room for residual calibration risk.
Combined with the controls, this separates the calibrated reliability signal
from a purely hand-written validity script: rules define checkable families,
but \pgeom{} must also separate GT positives from counterfactual negatives and
improve source rankings under the recall-violation metric.

Structured audit and visual sanity checks support the failure interpretation,
but they are not reported as large-scale independent human evaluation. The
structured audit covers 250 rows and reaches quality-issue precision of
0.8933. A reduced 50-row internal visual spot-check reaches a target-bucket
quality-issue rate of 0.9333 with contradiction rate 0.0333. These checks are
used to detect annotation or verifier issues and to support failure-mechanism
interpretation, not as primary metric evidence or as a blinded human audit.

Together, Table~\ref{tab:source-results} and the control/verifier checks
support a calibrated reliability-layer interpretation. They do not show that
H001 is a new relation generator, that it improves all open-vocabulary relation
labels, or that it is baseline-agnostic across arbitrary 3DSSG predictors.
Those blocked extensions are fixed in Table~\ref{tab:claim-boundary}. The
Open3DSG evidence remains meaningful despite the recovery-policy caveat because
all comparisons are made within the same selected official non-averaged
checkpoint, row contract, full-validation denominator, and recovered source
payload; the paper evaluates whether a reliability layer changes relation
rankings, not whether it establishes a new Open3DSG leaderboard result.

The qualitative failure analysis explains why the Open3DSG metric changes
occur. The full-validation Open3DSG branch produces 82,155 real
failure-analysis rows with zero validation errors, and the inspected 36-case
queue shows that 25 cases are demoted by geometry-aware re-ranking.
Figure~\ref{fig:failures} shows family-structured
failures: proximity failures often involve distance contradictions,
relative-vertical failures involve vertical-order contradictions, and
support/contact failures expose float-gap or support-plane contradictions.
These are the kinds of symbolic relation mistakes that may make graph edges
unreliable inputs for downstream reasoning, grounding, alignment, or embodied
decision modules, motivating downstream evaluation rather than establishing it
here.

The same qualitative analysis also exposes a limitation. Eight of the 36 sampled
rule-violated cases still have $\pgeom > 0.9$. This means the calibrated score
should not be interpreted as a hard validity label. It also justifies why the
paper reports probabilistic, rule-verified, and family-specific variants
separately rather than collapsing them into a single geometry-corrected output.
