Table~\ref{tab:source-results} asks whether calibrated geometry consistency
reduces violations without collapsing recall. The Open3DSG source exposes the
failure most clearly: semantic-only ranking places many geometrically
inconsistent edges at the top of the list, especially for the low-$K$
diagnostic. GeoCalib changes that operating point rather than merely pruning
edges. The calibrated product at $K=100$ improves recall by 8.86 percentage points over the
semantic source and reduces violation by 9.01 points; at $K=5$ and $K=10$, it
also raises recall while moving violation from the high-error semantic regime
to the calibrated-risk regime. Pooled risk reduces violations but is weaker
than the product on this tradeoff, and the strict-rule row
is a zero-violation diagnostic rather than the default method.

VL-SAT provides the controlled anchor. Its semantic ranking is already close to
ceiling, so the expected room for recall improvement is small. GeoCalib still
reduces violation consistently across the reported $K$ grid and gives a
positive \rAt{100} shift. The pooled ablation slightly favors recall at the
largest $K$ but leaves more violation, so we report it as a recall-favoring
ablation rather than a soft framework instantiation.

A 1,000-resample Docker subgraph bootstrap supports the same reading:
Open3DSG has positive recall and negative violation shifts across $K$, while
near-ceiling VL-SAT has smaller stable violation reductions. Main tables retain
the standard fixed-source top-$K$ point estimates.

No fusion dominates retrospectively: product has lower V on Open3DSG, while
rank fusion is stronger on VL-SAT at $K=100$. On frozen SGFN, product changes
R/V from 0.9235/0.0630 to 0.9416/0.0381 (paired deltas 0.0181
[0.0134,0.0233] and $-0.0249$ [$-0.0270$,$-0.0229$]). Rank-average reaches
0.9476/0.0277; versus product its recall CI crosses zero and V CI is negative.
This supports multiple fusion forms, not product superiority.

The aggregate effect is not family-uniform. On SGFN, product
\texttt{support\_contact} verifier V increases by 0.00450 (95\% CI
[0.00370,0.00532]), \texttt{proximity} is unchanged, and
\texttt{relative\_vertical} supplies most of the reduction. The same
support/contact direction appears in the retrospective sources. We therefore
claim aggregate scoped reliability and expose the family decomposition rather
than claiming every-family improvement.

Table~\ref{tab:controls} rules out several simpler alternatives.
Calibrator-only is not pooled calibrated re-ranking: it removes source
confidence entirely, although it still contains $T$ and $T\times G$.
Distance-only is weaker, and shuffled/wrong-pair controls show that object-pair
identity is necessary. Thus \method{} is not merely ranking by a calibrator,
distance, or hard rules. A strict 1,061/117/157 scan train/dev/final audit adds
true raw-$G$, $T$-only/additive/interaction baselines, and counterfactual
controls: correct-$T$ wins 97.44\% of 390 vertical GT rows; close-by swap and
vertical inverse errors are 0.01542/0.00124; and the correct--wrong-pair gap is
$+0.42341$ over 3,961 rows. This completes the factor audit, but neither solves
support/contact nor learns an evidence router.

The GT-based verifier check tests the geometry signal independently: GT
positives remain nonviolated at 0.9965, counterfactual negatives are
nonsatisfied at 0.9673, and \pgeom{} separates 7,944 rows with AUROC/AUPRC
0.9772/0.9729 and Brier 0.0543. This distinguishes the calibrated reliability
signal from a purely hand-written validity script.

Codex proxy passes yield valid/invalid/ambiguous/unobservable counts of
180/185/120/3 and 175/178/132/3. Agreement is 438/488 (89.75\%, four-class
$\kappa=0.845$); all 334 jointly binary items match, and every disagreement
touches ambiguity. This is blinded same-system stability, not Human V@K or
ground truth; earlier structured/visual checks remain supplementary.

Together, the metrics support a reliability layer, not a new generator or
broad SOTA claim. Open3DSG comparisons share one checkpoint, row contract, and
denominator. The historical 388/388 sensitivity branch changes old 377/388
\rAt{100} by only +0.28 points; it stays outside the main table, with its
teardown exit-137 caveat preserved in provenance.

Failure analysis explains why the Open3DSG metrics change. The full-validation
branch produces 82,155 diagnostic rows with zero validation errors; in the
36-case queue, 25 cases are demoted by geometry-aware re-ranking.
Figure~\ref{fig:failures} shows distance, vertical-order, and support-plane
contradictions that make symbolic edges unreliable for downstream reasoning.

The same analysis exposes residual risk: ten of 36 sampled rule-violated cases
still have $\pgeom > 0.9$. Thus \pgeom{} is not a hard validity label, and the
paper reports both soft fusion instantiations alongside pooled, RRF, and
rule-verified variants.
