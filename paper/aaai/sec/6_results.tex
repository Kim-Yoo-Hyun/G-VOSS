\subsection{Cross-Source Recall--Violation Trade-off}

Table~\ref{tab:source-results} asks whether calibrated geometry consistency
reduces violations without collapsing recall. The Open3DSG source exposes the
failure most clearly: source-score ranking places many geometrically
inconsistent edges at the top of the list, especially for the low-$K$
diagnostic. GeoCalib changes that operating point rather than merely pruning
edges. The family-calibrated product at $K=100$ improves recall by 8.86
percentage points over the source baseline and reduces violation by 9.01
points; at $K=5$ and $K=10$, it also raises recall while moving violation from the high-error source-score regime
to the calibrated-compatibility regime. Rank-average obtains nearly identical
recall with a higher V, while RRF attains the highest recall but leaves more
violations. The pooled product is weaker than family conditioning on this
tradeoff, and hard-rule filtering is a zero-violation diagnostic rather than
the default method.

VL-SAT provides the controlled anchor. Its source ranking is already close to
ceiling, so the expected room for recall improvement is small. GeoCalib still
reduces violation consistently across the reported $K$ grid and gives a
positive \rAt{100} shift. RRF is strongest at $K=100$ on both recall and V,
whereas rank-average sacrifices low-$K$ recall. This source dependence is why
we report all fusion rules rather than selecting a universal winner.

The lower-budget results sharpen the formula boundary. On SGFN, the calibrated
product improves both metrics at $K=10$, $50$, and $100$, whereas rank-average
and RRF sacrifice recall at $K=10$ and $50$. Thus the claim that two
evaluated fusions satisfy the joint criterion is specific to the primary $K=100$
endpoint; persistence across smaller budgets is supported by the calibrated
product, not by every instantiation.

A 1,000-resample paired context bootstrap supports the same reading:
Open3DSG has positive recall and negative violation shifts across $K$, while
near-ceiling VL-SAT has smaller stable violation reductions. Main tables retain
the standard fixed-source top-$K$ point estimates.

An uncertainty sensitivity check rules out a denominator artifact. At $K=100$,
the family product reduces decidable-only V by .0244/.1671/.0428 on
VL-SAT/Open3DSG/SGFN and also lowers the uncertainty rate on every source. The
pessimistic definition that counts every uncertain row as violated remains
lower by .0480/.2526/.0586; all paired 95\% CIs exclude zero. Full status counts,
coverage, and intervals are reported in the supplement.

\subsection{Source Generalization, Learned Rescoring, and Family Effects}

No fusion dominates across the observed sources: the family product has lower
V on Open3DSG, while rank fusion is stronger on VL-SAT at $K=100$. On SGFN,
the family product changes
R/V from 0.9235/0.0630 to 0.9416/0.0381 (paired deltas 0.0181
[0.0134,0.0233] and $-0.0249$ [$-0.0270$,$-0.0229$]). Rank-average reaches
0.9476/0.0277; versus product its recall CI crosses zero and V CI is negative.
This supports multiple fusion forms, not product superiority.

The parameter-matched nonlinear comparison is stronger when source-specific
labels are available. It reaches R/V of 0.5441/0.0120 at $K=10$,
0.8681/0.0186 at $K=50$, and 0.9466/0.0279 at $K=100$. At $K=100$, its recall
advantage over the family product is not significant, but its V reduction is;
at smaller budgets it improves both. This rules out formula optimality and
shows the value of supervised source adaptation. It does not explain away the
source-independent setting addressed by GeoCalib: the MLP consumes exact SGFN
labels, while the proposed compatibility factor is fitted without source
identity, source scores, or source-specific correctness labels.

The aggregate effect is not family-uniform. On SGFN, the family product's
support/contact verifier V increases by 0.00450 (95\% CI
[0.00370,0.00532]), proximity is unchanged, and relative-vertical relations
supply most of the reduction. The same
support/contact direction appears in the retrospective sources. We therefore
claim aggregate scoped reliability and expose the family decomposition rather
than claiming every-family improvement. The supplement reports within-family
and actual global-top-$K$ slices separately, so this conclusion does not depend
on hiding a change in family composition.

\subsection{Controls and Validity Checks}

Table~\ref{tab:controls} rules out several simpler alternatives.
Compatibility-only is not pooled re-ranking: it removes the source score,
although it still contains $T$ and $T\times G$. Distance-only is weaker, and
shuffled/mismatched-pair controls show that object-pair identity is necessary.
Thus \method{} is not merely ranking by a compatibility score,
distance, or hard rules. A strict split audit adds true raw-$G$, $T$-only,
additive, interaction, and counterfactual controls. Correct-predicate
compatibility wins on 97.44\% of 390 vertical ground-truth rows,
endpoint/inverse-equivariance errors remain below 1.6\%, and correct pairs
exceed mismatched pairs by 0.423 over 3,961 rows. The audit does not imply that
support/contact is solved.

The ground-truth/counterfactual check isolates the geometry signal from source
re-ranking:
ground-truth positives remain nonviolated at 0.9965, counterfactual negatives are
nonsatisfied at 0.9673, and \pgeom{} separates 7,944 rows with AUROC/AUPRC
0.9772/0.9729 and Brier 0.0543. This shows that soft compatibility is not
identical to hard filtering, while remaining a verifier-derived diagnostic.

\subsection{Qualitative Failures}

In the 36-case Open3DSG queue, 25 relations are demoted by geometry-aware
re-ranking. Figure~\ref{fig:failures} grounds two such distance/support
contradictions and one residual error: the door--floor relation receives
$C\approx1$ and moves from rank 21 to 10 despite a verifier violation.
Compatibility is therefore a soft factor, not a validity label.
