\subsection{Relation-Consistent Compatibility}

For each candidate relation $r_i$, the associated geometry vector $G_i$ contains
OBB-derived 3D/XY distances, relative height, projected overlap, and
vertical-gap features. Point-level contact evidence is used only by the
support/contact verifier, not by the compatibility model. Let
$\operatorname{std}_{\rm tr}$ standardize each numeric feature with
training-split statistics and replace a missing value by the corresponding
training mean. We evaluate two compact compatibility estimators under the same
input separation, training targets, loss, and transformation averaging.

\paragraph{Linear estimator.}
The feature map for \methodlinear{} is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_{\rm lin}(T_i,G_i)&=[1;\phi_T(T_i);\operatorname{std}_{\rm tr}(G_i);
\operatorname{std}_{\rm tr}(\phi_{\times}(T_i,G_i))],\\
\phi_{\times}(T_i,G_i)&=
[d(p_i)\Delta z_i,\ d(p_i)\Delta z_i^{\rm norm}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\phi_T$ contains the predicate indicator, and $d(p_i)$ is $1$ for
\texttt{higher than}, $-1$ for \texttt{lower than}, and $0$ otherwise; hence the
two explicit interaction terms are active only for vertical-order predicates.
The family label selects a linear head but is not repeated as a constant input:
$f_{\rm lin}(T_i,a_i,G_i)=w_{a_i}^\top\Phi_{\rm lin}(T_i,G_i)$.

\paragraph{Nonlinear estimator.}
\methodmlp{} uses one shared two-hidden-unit ReLU network. Its input $\Psi_i$
contains family and predicate indicators, the same underlying geometric
measurements, the two signed-height interactions above, and the sum of the two
directional overlap ratios. With a predicate-linear skip connection, its logit is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h_i&=\operatorname{ReLU}(W\Psi_i+b),\\
f_{\rm mlp}(T_i,a_i,G_i)&=v^\top h_i+b_o+
\beta^\top\phi_T(T_i).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The nonlinear estimator is shared across families, so its family indicator is
not a constant feature. Both estimators exclude the predictor score, rank,
predictor identity, and object-class features.

For proximity, $\tau$ swaps the ordered endpoints and leaves \texttt{close by}
unchanged. For vertical order, $\tau$ swaps the endpoints and maps
\texttt{higher than}$\leftrightarrow$\texttt{lower than}. Transformation
averaging therefore gives
$C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm close}(s,o)=C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm close}(o,s)$ and
$C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm higher}(s,o)=C^{\rm tr,q}_{\rm lower}(o,s)$.

Training combines transformation-consistent augmentation with a linked
positive--counterfactual ranking loss. The augmentation preserves the predicate when proximity endpoints
are swapped, whereas vertical order swaps endpoints and applies the inverse
predicate. In addition,
for every linked positive--counterfactual pair $(i^+,i^-)\in\mathcal P$, the
logits $\ell_i^q=f_q(T_i,a_i,G_i)$ receive a fixed margin penalty:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal L_q={}&\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}
+0.25\,\mathbb E_{\mathcal P}\!
 \left[\log\!\left(1+e^{1-(\ell^q_{i^+}-\ell^q_{i^-})}\right)\right]\\
&+10^{-4}\mathcal R(\theta_q).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal L_{\rm BCE}$ is binary cross-entropy over positive and
counterfactual examples. The second term is a soft margin-ranking loss with
margin $m=1$; its weight $0.25$ and the $\ell_2$ coefficient $10^{-4}$ are
shared across estimators and predictors without predictor-specific search, and
$\mathcal R$ penalizes non-bias weights. The
supplement reports sensitivity, optimization details, and training-example
counts. Both estimators are fitted exclusively on the training split.

Finally, let $H_{a_i}$ denote the finite transformation group for family
$a_i$, and define the transformation orbit
$\mathcal O_i=\{g(T_i,G_i):g\in H_{a_i}\}$. We average the learned score over
this set to obtain the transformation-consistent compatibility:
\begin{equation}
C_i^{\rm tr,q}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal O_i|}
\sum_{(T',G')\in\mathcal O_i} \sigma\!\left(f_q(T',a_i,G')\right).
\label{eq:algebra-projection}
\end{equation}
Group averaging makes proximity symmetry and invariance under joint endpoint
swap and inverse-predicate transformation exact at inference. It does not use
the predictor score, while the pairwise objective still orders each linked
positive above its counterfactual. The supplement provides a formal statement
and proof. For the Product (all families) comparison, $H_a=\{\mathrm{id}\}$
for support/contact.

\subsection{Family-Aware Re-ranking}

For either compatibility estimator $q$, define the within-family ranking score
\begin{equation}
u_i^q=\begin{cases}
Z_iC_i^{\rm tr,q}, & a_i\in\mathcal A_{\rm rank},\\
Z_i, & a_i=\mathrm{support/contact}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
For positive factors, $\log u_i^q=\log Z_i+\log C_i^{\rm tr,q}$ in the
families with defined transformations; compatibility contributes an additive
log-score term without a fitted fusion parameter.

Let $\pi^Z=(e_1,\ldots,e_n)$ denote the source ranking and $a_j$ the relation
family at position $j$. For each $a\in\mathcal A_{\rm rank}$, let $\pi_a^q$ contain
that family's candidates sorted by $u_i^q$, with ties resolved deterministically
by exact relation-candidate identity. For support/contact, $\pi_a^q$ is defined
directly as the corresponding subsequence of $\pi^Z$ for both estimators. At position $j$, the rule
selects the next unused candidate from the corresponding family list, exactly preserving the
source family-label sequence. Its support/contact subsequence and
family-specific Recall and Violation are therefore unchanged at every $K$. For
each prefix, the rule also selects the highest-scoring candidates within each
re-ranked family and maximizes their summed ranking score subject to the preserved
family counts and support/contact subsequence; the supplement gives an exchange
proof.

\methodlinear{} and \methodmlp{} share the factor separation, training targets,
loss, transformations, product ranking score, and family-aware ranking procedure; they
differ in parameterization and nonlinear capacity. Rank-fusion baselines use the
same ranking procedure with alternative score combinations. Additional controls test the predictor score, pair
identity, geometry, and the defined transformations; full definitions are
provided in the experiments and supplement.
