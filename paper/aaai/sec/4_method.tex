\section{Method}

\method{} is a reliability layer for relation predictions, not a replacement
3D Scene Graph generator. It preserves candidate identity, measures
predicate-conditioned geometry compatibility independently of the source
relation score, and combines the two signals only at re-ranking time.
Figure~\ref{fig:framework} gives the complete pipeline.

\subsection{Relation Rows and Geometry Evidence}

We convert each source prediction to a common row
\begin{equation}
(\mathrm{scan},\mathrm{context},s,o,p,a,Z),
\end{equation}
where $s$ and $o$ are ordered instance identifiers, $p$ is the predicate,
$a$ its relation family, and $Z$ the original source relation score. The ordered
key $(\mathrm{scan},\mathrm{context},s,o)$ joins each candidate to the geometry
of the same object pair. It prevents category-level plausibility or unordered
pair statistics from substituting for instance-level evidence.

The raw geometry vector $G$ is predicate-independent. It contains pair
distance and scale, centroid and extent differences, oriented-box overlap, and
point-level contact/support evidence when observable. Predicate semantics are
represented by $T=(p,a)$. We measure three families whose constraints can be
tested from the reconstruction: support/contact, proximity, and relative
vertical order. Family-specific transformations interpret $G$ for $T$---for
example, the sign of vertical displacement for \texttt{higher than} versus
\texttt{lower than}---but neither source score nor source identity enters the
geometry representation. Insufficient evidence is retained as uncertain
rather than forced into a valid/invalid label.

\subsection{Compatibility Calibration and Re-ranking}

For each family $a$, we fit a logistic compatibility calibrator
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
C_i&=\sigma\!\left(w_{a_i}^{\top}\Phi(T_i,G_i)\right)
   \approx P(y_i^{cal}=1\mid T_i,G_i),\\
Z_i&\notin\operatorname{inputs}(C_i),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\Phi=(\phi_T,\phi_G,\phi_{\times})$. The interaction block
$\phi_{\times}$ contains explicit predicate--geometry transformations, while
$\phi_G$ remains predicate-independent. The calibration target uses 2,565
train/dev ground-truth positives and 3,244 high-margin counterfactual
negatives; it is a constructed compatibility target, not human physical
validity. We standardize numeric features with training statistics and minimize
binary cross-entropy with $L_2=10^{-4}$ for 800 fixed gradient steps
(learning rate 0.2). The three family models use 22--24 features and no source
score or source identity. Training/dev rows, feature groups, and held-out
calibration diagnostics are detailed in the supplement.

Our first instantiation preserves score magnitudes through a
family-calibrated product,
\begin{equation}
S_i^{\mathrm{prod}}=Z_i C_i.
\end{equation}
Equivalently, $\log S_i^{\mathrm{prod}}=\log Z_i+\log C_i$ applies a soft
compatibility penalty with fixed unit weight. Our second instantiation avoids
cross-source scale assumptions. Let $q_i^Z$ and $q_i^C$ be descending
within-context percentile ranks; then
\begin{equation}
S_i^{\mathrm{rank}}=\tfrac{1}{2}(q_i^Z+q_i^C).
\end{equation}
The equal weight is parameter-free. These are complementary framework
instantiations rather than a
claim that either fusion is universally optimal.

\subsection{Comparisons and Falsification Controls}

We compare against the original source relation score, a pooled-compatibility
product $Z_i\pgeom_i$, fixed-constant Reciprocal Rank Fusion, and a hard-rule
filter. We additionally fit a source-supervised nonlinear rescorer on the
117-scan internal-development source. It consumes $Z$, the complete $T/G$
factorization, and the two directed interactions through a two-hidden-unit ReLU
MLP with a predicate-linear skip. Its 69 trainable parameters match the total
22+23+24 coefficients of the three family compatibility calibrators. This is
an advantaged comparison: unlike GeoCalib, it uses exact source-specific labels
and is not a source-independent replacement. The pooled model tests whether
family conditioning is necessary; the
hard-rule result is a diagnostic operating point rather than a proposed
zero-violation solution. A compatibility-only control removes $Z$, and a
generic distance heuristic tests whether calibration is needed at all.
Shuffled-geometry and mismatched-pair controls break the identity join while
preserving marginal geometry statistics. A separate factor audit evaluates
$T$-only, true $G$-only, additive $T+G$, and interaction $T\times G$ models,
together with wrong-predicate, endpoint-swap where defined, and vertical
inverse-equivariance tests. These controls make the central claim falsifiable:
the gain should depend on the correct predicate, ordered object pair, and
geometry---not on score leakage or a generic distance prior.
