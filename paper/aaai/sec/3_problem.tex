\section{Method}

\method{} is a post-source reliability layer rather than a replacement 3D
Scene Graph generator. It preserves relation-candidate identity, estimates
predicate-conditioned geometric compatibility without access to source
confidence, and combines the two factors only during re-ranking.
Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the pipeline.

\subsection{Problem Setup and Reliability Objective}

Let a relation source produce candidate rows
\begin{equation}
r_i=(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i,p_i,a_i,Z_i),
\end{equation}
where $s_i$ and $o_i$ are ordered subject and object instance identifiers,
$p_i$ is the predicate, $a_i$ its mapped relation family, and $Z_i$ the source
relation score. A context is the source-defined ranking unit associated with one
scene subgraph; top-$K$ selection is performed independently within each
context. The key
$(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i)$ preserves the same object pair
across prediction, geometry, and ground-truth joins. We expose
predicate/family semantics as $T_i=(p_i,a_i)$ and join raw pair geometry
\begin{equation}
G_i=G(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i).
\end{equation}
The measured scope is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathrm{support/contact},\ \mathrm{proximity},\
\ \mathrm{vertical\ order}\},
\end{equation}
whose constraints are identifiable from reconstructed ordered-pair geometry.
Rows outside these families are excluded from the measured violation claim.
The primary routing scope is narrower:
$\mathcal A_{\rm alg}=\{\mathrm{proximity},\mathrm{vertical\ order}\}$,
for which the declared relation algebra is exact. Support/contact is evaluated
but passed through because its current evidence lacks a valid family-wide
endpoint transform.

Our objective is to estimate a bounded compatibility score
\begin{equation}
C_i=\sigma\!\left(h_{a_i}(\Phi(T_i,G_i))\right)\in[0,1],\qquad
Z_i\notin\operatorname{inputs}(C_i),
\end{equation}
then enforce the applicable relation algebra to obtain $\calg_i$ and form a
final ranking score $S_i=F(Z_i,\calg_i)$. The score measures compatibility with
a training target comprising ground-truth positives and counterfactual
negatives; it is not a probability of physical validity. Predicate-aligned
transformations are $T_i\times G_i$ interactions rather than raw geometry.
Neither source score nor predictor identity enters $C_i$, so one model can be
applied unchanged to different predictors on the shared target.

Let $L_K(c)$ be the top-$K$ measured-family rows for context $c$ and $Y_c$ its
exact-label ground-truth set. We jointly report
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{R@K}&=\frac{\sum_c |L_K(c)\cap Y_c|}{\sum_c |Y_c|},\\
\mathrm{Verifier\mbox{-}V@K}&=\frac{1}{|D_K|}
\sum_{(c,i)\in D_K}\mathbf{1}[v_i=\mathrm{violated}].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $D_K$ contains selected rows with a supported verifier status
$v_i\in\{\mathrm{satisfied},\mathrm{uncertain},\mathrm{violated}\}$.
Family mapping never relaxes exact predicate matching. Because uncertain rows
can lower the all-status ratio without being declared satisfied, the
experiments also report decidable-only violation, uncertainty, pessimistic
violation, and coverage.
