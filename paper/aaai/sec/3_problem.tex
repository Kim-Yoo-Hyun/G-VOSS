\section{Method}

\method{} assesses fixed relation predictions rather than replacing a 3D Scene
Graph generator. It preserves candidate identity, estimates predicate--geometry
compatibility without the predictor score, and combines the two only
during re-ranking (Figure~\ref{fig:framework}).

\subsection{Problem Setup}

Let a relation predictor produce relation candidates
$r_i=(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i,p_i,a_i,Z_i)$, where $s_i$
and $o_i$ are ordered subject and object instance identifiers,
$p_i$ is the predicate, $a_i$ its mapped relation family, and $Z_i$ the
predictor's native relation-ranking score. We use sigmoid relation scores for
VL-SAT and SceneGraphFusion (SGFN), and normalized text-embedding cosine
similarity for Open3DSG
\cite{wang2023vlsat,wu2021scenegraphfusion,koch2024open3dsg},
without predictor-specific normalization or refitting. Predictors are ranked
separately; scores are never compared across predictors. A context is a scene subgraph over
which top-$K$ selection is performed independently.
We distinguish the ordered-pair identity
$k_i^{\rm pair}=(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i)$ from the exact
relation-candidate identity
$k_i^{\rm rel}=(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,p_i,o_i)$ used for
evaluation. The ordered-pair identity is retained when associating each
prediction with geometry and ground truth. We represent predicate semantics as
$T_i=p_i$. The family label $a_i$ always selects the transformation set and
re-ranking scope. It also selects the head and training-split normalization
statistics for the linear estimator, whereas the shared nonlinear estimator
encodes it as a semantic indicator. Each candidate is associated with
predicate-independent ordered-pair measurements
$G_i=G(\mathrm{scan}_i,\mathrm{context}_i,s_i,o_i)$. The reported evaluation
scope is $\mathcal{A}_{\rm eval}=\{\mathrm{support/contact},\mathrm{proximity},
\mathrm{vertical\ order}\}$, whose consistency can be assessed from the
geometry of the reconstructed ordered object pair. Candidates outside these
families are excluded from the reported Recall and Violation evaluation.
The re-ranking scope is narrower:
    $\mathcal A_{\rm rank}=\{\mathrm{proximity},\mathrm{vertical\ order}\}$,
    for which the defined endpoint/predicate transformations are exact.
Support/contact is evaluated but kept in source order because this family
requires richer local contact and pose evidence, and no single endpoint
transformation preserves the semantics of every predicate in the family.

For estimator $q\in\{\mathrm{lin},\mathrm{mlp}\}$, our objective is to estimate
a bounded compatibility score
\begin{equation}
C_i^q=\sigma\!\left(f_q(T_i,a_i,G_i)\right)\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
then average over the valid family-specific endpoint/predicate transformations
to obtain the transformation-consistent score $C_i^{\mathrm{tr},q}$. The
predictor score enters only through the within-family ranking score defined
below. The compatibility and ranking scores are used for ordering and are not
interpreted as posterior probabilities of physical validity.

Training-split ground truth provides positives. Proximity negatives use distant,
non-overlapping same-context pairs; vertical negatives invert the predicate on
the same pair; support/contact negatives replace one endpoint under separation
margins. Separately, relation-preserving augmentation swaps proximity endpoints
without changing the predicate and jointly swaps vertical endpoints and the
inverse predicate. All training examples come from the training split;
evaluation rows, predictor scores, and verifier-status labels are excluded,
although the construction rules and primary verifier share some
oriented-bounding-box (OBB)-derived measurements. Full construction rules are
provided in the supplement.
