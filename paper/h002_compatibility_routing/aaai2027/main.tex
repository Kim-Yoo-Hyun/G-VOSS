\begin{abstract}
Confidence from a 3D Scene Graph relation predictor need not measure whether a
candidate relation agrees with the reconstructed geometry. We address this gap
with a source-output reliability layer that separates predicate and object
semantics $\Te$, predicate-independent pair geometry $\Ge$, and source
confidence $\Ze$. A train-only compatibility model computes
$C_e^{\mathrm{raw}}=f_\theta(\Te,\Ge)$ without access to $\Ze$; source
confidence enters only through the final risk-aware product
$\Scomp=\widetilde Z_e\widetilde C_e$. This factorization makes the mechanism
testable with wrong-predicate, shuffled-geometry, and geometry-only controls.
On VL-SAT and Open3DSG predictions over the official 3DSSG validation split,
$\Scomp$ improves the comparison-route Recall@20 from 0.6429 to 0.7245 while
reducing Violation@20 from 0.3436 to 0.1005; grouped bootstrap intervals exclude
zero for both changes. The same joint improvement holds at K=10 and K=50. A
frame-aware left/right route consistently reduces violations but exhibits a
source-dependent recall tradeoff. These results establish predicate-geometry
compatibility for geometry-checkable comparison relations and expose where
geometry-only, reference-frame, contact/pose, or observability-specific evidence
is instead required. The study is validation-level and does not claim an
all-relation or hidden-test solution.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

3D Scene Graphs provide an explicit interface between geometric perception and
semantic reasoning \cite{armeni2019scenegraph,wald2020learning,
wu2021scenegraphfusion}. Relation predictors such as VL-SAT and Open3DSG assign
scores to candidate edges, yet these scores are optimized for prediction and
ranking, not for relation-level geometric reliability
\cite{wang2023vlsat,koch2024open3dsg}. A high-confidence edge can therefore be
semantically plausible while contradicting the observed object-pair geometry.

The failure cannot be resolved by attaching one geometry score to every
predicate. For a fixed pair geometry, \emph{higher than} and \emph{lower than}
require opposite interpretations; \emph{close by} can be decided largely from
distance; and support, attachment, or containment require contact, pose, or
observability evidence that scalar pair geometry may not provide. This
heterogeneity motivates relation-aware evidence routing rather than a universal
semantic-geometry fusion rule.

We instantiate the geometry-checkable compatibility route by separating
semantic content $\Te$, predicate-independent geometry $\Ge$, and source
confidence $\Ze$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:score-flow}). The compatibility model
$C_e^{\mathrm{raw}}=f_\theta(\Te,\Ge)$ is trained without $\Ze$; only after
compatibility is computed do we combine the normalized terms as
$\Scomp=\widetilde Z_e\widetilde C_e$. The separation creates a concrete
identifiability test: performance should collapse when predicates or geometries
are mismatched, and it should not be explained by geometry-only or generic
concatenation baselines.

Across VL-SAT and Open3DSG validation predictions, compatibility reranking
improves both semantic utility and geometric consistency on higher/lower and
bigger/smaller relations. At K=20, Recall rises by 8.16 points and Violation
falls by 24.31 points, with grouped bootstrap intervals excluding zero. The
left/right analysis further shows that reference-frame evidence can lower
violations while producing source-dependent recall tradeoffs. We do not solve
reliable 3D relation estimation across all relation families; instead, the
failure analyses delineate the evidence needed to extend beyond the validated
comparison route.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item We formulate source-output relation reliability as a factorized,
route-aware problem and isolate predicate-geometry compatibility from source
confidence.
\item We introduce a leakage-controlled compatibility reranker with a
parameter-free risk-aware product and counterfactual identifiability controls.
\item We provide a two-source validation study with grouped confidence
intervals, mechanism ablations, a qualified lateral route, and explicit failure
taxonomies for unsupported relation families.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Scene graphs and 3D relation prediction.}
Scene graphs encode objects and their relations for retrieval and visual
reasoning \cite{johnson2015image,krishna2017visualgenome}. Context modeling,
message passing, and debiasing have driven 2D SGG
\cite{xu2017iterative,zellers2018neuralmotifs,yang2018graphrcnn,
tang2020unbiased}. In 3D, hierarchical scene graphs and 3DSSG ground semantics
in reconstructed space \cite{armeni2019scenegraph,wald2019rio,wald2020learning},
while later systems predict and update relations from point clouds or RGB-D
streams \cite{wu2021scenegraphfusion,wu2023rgbsgg,zhang2021knowledge3d}.
VL-SAT strengthens supervised 3DSSG prediction with visual-linguistic training
\cite{wang2023vlsat}; Open3DSG and ConceptGraphs broaden the source vocabulary
\cite{koch2024open3dsg,gu2024conceptgraphs}. Our method operates after these source
models and does not replace their candidate generation.

\paragraph{Geometric evidence for relations.}
Point-cloud architectures such as PointNet and PointNet++ provide learned 3D
representations \cite{qi2017pointnet,qi2017pointnetpp}, and online 3D graph
systems already exploit geometry for segmentation, neighborhood construction,
and relation prediction \cite{wu2021scenegraphfusion,hou2025fross}. RelWitness
further studies visual-geometric evidence that makes open-vocabulary relations
observable \cite{nguyen2026relwitness}. Our focus is complementary: rather than
using geometry only as another predictor input, we isolate a source-independent
compatibility variable and test whether matched predicate-pair geometry improves
the reliability of an existing ranked list.

\paragraph{Calibration and conditional evidence.}
Calibration measures whether confidence tracks correctness
\cite{guo2017calibration,nixon2019calibration}, while selective prediction
studies when insufficient evidence should trigger rejection
\cite{geifman2017selective,geifman2019selectivenet}. Gated fusion and conditional
computation allow models to vary which evidence is used
\cite{shazeer2017moe,perez2018film,arevalo2017gmu}. Our method specializes these ideas
to relation-level 3D evidence: it separates source confidence from
predicate-geometry compatibility and uses route analysis to identify where the
validated mechanism applies and where additional evidence is required.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Factorized Compatibility}

Let $e=(s,p,o)$ denote a candidate edge produced by a relation source. We
separate its information into
\[
\Te=f_T(p,c_s,c_o),\qquad
\Ge=f_G(x_s,x_o),
\]
\[
\Ze=(s_{\mathrm{src}},r_{\mathrm{src}}),
\]
where $\Te$ contains predicate and endpoint semantics, $\Ge$ contains
predicate-independent geometry for the ordered object pair, and $\Ze$ contains
the source score and rank. The central constraint is
\[
C_e^{\mathrm{raw}}=f_\theta(\Te,\Ge),\qquad
\Ze\notin f_\theta .
\]
This makes compatibility a distinct evidence channel rather than a learned copy
of source confidence.

Each edge is stored in three aligned views: a model-safe $\Te/\Ge$ view, a
reranking-only $\Ze$ view, and a hidden metric view containing GT matches,
geometric status, and construction metadata. The latter is never exposed to the
model. Compatibility supervision is constructed on the training split. Positive
anchors are exact GT relations that satisfy the family geometry contract;
negative examples preserve the scene, object pair, family, or rank stratum while
changing the predicate or selecting a geometrically incompatible alternative.
For size and directional comparisons, same-geometry predicate flips provide the
hardest counterfactuals. All rows derived from the same scene/object-pair group
remain in one split.

The current $f_\theta$ is an L2-regularized logistic model over one-hot semantic
features, raw geometry, and explicit predicate-geometry interactions. These
interactions include predicate-signed vertical offsets, size ratios, and
frame-aware horizontal offsets. This deliberately small model isolates the
compatibility mechanism: geometry-only and plain $\Te+\Ge$ models use the same
training rows but omit the matched interaction terms. Wrong-predicate,
shuffled-geometry, and wrong-pair evaluations intervene on one factor while
holding the other fixed.

\subsection{Relation-Aware Reranking}

Relation families are assigned by the evidence needed to judge them. We ask
whether metric geometry is sufficient, whether the predicate changes its
interpretation, whether a reference frame is required, and whether local
contact, pose, observability, or structural evidence is missing. Table
\ref{tab:route-taxonomy} records the resulting method scope. It is an evidence
contract, not a claim that every route is solved.

The raw source and compatibility scores are normalized without labels over the
candidate pool:
\[
\widetilde Z_e=\mathrm{minmax}_{\mathrm{source}}(\Ze),
\]
\[
\widetilde C_e=\mathrm{minmax}_{\mathrm{source,family}}
(C_e^{\mathrm{raw}}).
\]
We compare the source baseline $\Ssrc(e)=\Ze$ with
\[
\Scomp(e)=\mathrm{clip}(\widetilde Z_e)
            \mathrm{clip}(\widetilde C_e).
\]
In log space, this product is the $\lambda=1$ case of
\[
\log \Scomp(e)=\log\widetilde Z_e-
\lambda[-\log\widetilde C_e].
\]
Thus compatibility acts as a geometric-risk penalty on source utility. We fix
$\lambda=1$ to avoid validation-tuned risk weighting and clamp both terms to
$[10^{-6},1]$ only for numerical stability. No GT or Violation label enters the
normalization bounds.

The main comparisons are source-only, compatibility-only, geometry-only, plain
concatenation, wrong predicate, and shuffled geometry. A useful compatibility
score must improve over the first four and collapse under factor mismatch. The
method reranks existing candidates; it does not generate missing relations or
replace the source predictor. Observability quality $\Qe$ is retained only as
an extension boundary: calibrated selective reliability is left for future
validation.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Data, Splits, and Sources}

Compatibility models are fit on 4,868 internal-train rows. The grouped split
also contains 1,044 development and 1,040 held-out diagnostic rows; scenes,
ordered pairs, and their counterfactual variants do not cross groups. Neither
diagnostic split is used to tune the final source-reranking weight. Official
validation contributes no fitting rows.

We evaluate 762,888 candidate edges from VL-SAT (441,696) and Open3DSG
(321,192) on the official 3DSSG validation split. Open3DSG remains an
open-vocabulary source, but Recall@K is computed after mapping predictions to
the closed 3DSSG predicate vocabulary. The experiment is a validation-split
study; no hidden-test label or evaluation server is used.

\subsection{Routes, Metrics, and Baselines}

The primary route contains higher/lower and bigger/smaller. Left/right is
evaluated separately with a frozen scene-frame scorer; front/behind is retained
for failure analysis rather than pooled into a horizontal average. We report
$K\in\{5,10,20,50,100\}$. Recall@K measures mapped GT recovery. For the
geometrically evaluable subset $E_K$ of a top-K list,
\[
\mathrm{Violation@K}=\frac{1}{|E_K|}
\sum_{e\in E_K}\mathbf{1}[v(e)=1].
\]
Violation@K is a custom reliability diagnostic, not an official 3DSSG metric.
We compare source-only, compatibility-only, geometry-only, plain concatenation,
wrong-predicate, and shuffled-compatibility scores. Confidence intervals use
1,000 grouped bootstrap resamples over 2,192 source/subgraph/route units.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Main Comparison Route}

Figure~\ref{fig:main-comparison} reports validation-split source reranking on
the comparison route. At K=10/20/50, Recall improves by
4.20/8.16/10.32 points and Violation decreases by 22.99/24.31/25.92 points;
all six grouped intervals exclude zero. K=5 shows a significant violation
reduction but its Recall interval crosses zero, while K=100 is near candidate
list saturation. The mid-K regime therefore provides the clearest joint
utility-reliability evidence.

\subsection{Failure and Boundary Routes}

Front/behind is kept as frame/depth ambiguity. In selected cells, violation is
reduced but recall losses are large, e.g., Open3DSG K = 50 has
$\Delta$Recall = -0.1704 and $\Delta$Violation = -0.3592, while VL-SAT K = 20
has $\Delta$Recall = -0.2031 and $\Delta$Violation = -0.2731.
Support/contact does not provide an independent reliability target under the
current evidence. The latest repair yields only 35 accept and 347 reject binary
rows, so a reject-only classifier already reaches 0.908 accuracy.
Moreover, the geometry-rule construction state recovers the binary target with
1.0 leave-one-out accuracy and macro recall. This is target circularity, not
evidence that support/contact reliability has been learned. Target circularity
prevents an independent support/contact evaluation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

\paragraph{Scope and failure modes.}
The quantitative evidence supports predicate-geometry compatibility for
higher/lower and bigger/smaller, with left/right as a source-dependent
frame-aware route. It does not establish one universal reliability function.
Close by is already geometry-decidable; front/behind remains sensitive to depth
and reference-frame choice; and support/contact requires independent labels plus
richer contact and pose evidence. Attachment and containment additionally depend
on whether relevant visual or mesh evidence is observable. The route analysis is
therefore a map of evidence requirements, not an all-family success claim.

\paragraph{Evaluation and model limitations.}
All results use the public 3DSSG validation split. Recall is evaluated through
the closed predicate mapping, and Violation@K is a custom geometric diagnostic.
The candidate-pool min--max transform is label-free but not invariant to score
normalization: raw-product sensitivity preserves the main direction at several
K values, whereas rank-percentile normalization loses low-K Recall. We also
tested learned geometry outside the main path; it improves some recall-oriented
slices but violates the frozen non-regression requirement. Consequently, the
learned geometry variant is not part of the primary score. The current model
also cannot recover a relation absent from the source candidate list.

\paragraph{Reproducibility.}
The release package will include source adapters, geometry extraction,
model-safe compatibility scoring, counterfactual controls, grouped bootstrap
evaluation, Docker commands, and compact tables. Dataset scans, source
checkpoints, and license-restricted row-level manifests remain external
dependencies. Open3DSG is used as an open-vocabulary source, while the reported
quantitative evaluation remains closed-vocabulary and validation-level.

\section{Conclusion}

We showed that source confidence and predicate-geometry compatibility are
distinct signals for 3D Scene Graph relations. A leakage-controlled
compatibility model improves Recall and reduces geometric violations across two
relation sources, and counterfactual controls verify that the gain depends on
matched predicate-pair geometry. The route analysis also identifies the limits
of this mechanism: frame ambiguity, contact/pose evidence, observability, and
target independence must be addressed before extending the claim beyond
geometry-checkable comparison relations.
